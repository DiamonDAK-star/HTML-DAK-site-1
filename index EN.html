Introduction

Bees play a key role in ecosystems, significantly affecting nature and humans. These amazing insects are the main pollinators of many plants, which contributes to the maintenance of biodiversity and the stability of natural communities. About 75% of all flowering plants depend on pollination by insects, among which bees occupy a prominent place. Pollination by bees ensures the formation of fruits and seeds, which are the basis for plant reproduction and the maintenance of food chains.

The importance of bees for humans

Bees are of great agricultural importance to humans. They pollinate many crops, including fruits, vegetables, and nuts, increasing the yield and quality of produce. Without their work, many products would be less accessible and more expensive. According to the FAO (Food and Agriculture Organization of the United Nations), pollination by insects, including bees, provides about 35% of the world's agricultural production.

Threats to bees

Climate change

Climate change is negatively affecting bees, reducing the availability of flowers for pollination, causing heat stress and the spread of diseases, which threatens their survival and productivity.




Air pollution

Air pollution negatively affects bees and damages their sense of smell, which reduces pollination of plants and endangers their survival.




Pesticides

Pesticides negatively affect bees, causing poisoning, disorientation, and reduced immunity, which leads to a decrease in their population and a threat to plant pollination.


Habitat Loss

Habitat loss reduces the number of flowering plants and safe nesting sites for bees, leading to a decline in their population and pollination efficiency.
